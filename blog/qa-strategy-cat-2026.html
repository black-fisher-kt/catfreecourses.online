QA Strategy for CAT 2026: Topic Weightage & High-Score Preparation Plan

Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

QA is the most predictable section in CAT — the syllabus is fixed, topic-wise distribution stays consistent year on year, and almost every concept traces back to Class 9-10 maths. That predictability is your biggest advantage: unlike VARC or DILR, you can literally plan your prep around known weightage.

Topic-Wise Weightage

QA has around 22 questions in 40 minutes. Based on CAT 2020-2025 trends, here's how questions typically distribute — for the full section breakdown, see our CAT Syllabus 2026 guide.

TopicWeightageApprox. Questions
Arithmetic35-45%8-10
Algebra25-30%5-7
Geometry & Mensuration15%3-4
Number System8%1-2
Modern Maths7%1-2

Arithmetic and Algebra together make up 60-70% of the entire section. A student who masters just these two areas has already covered the majority of QA.

Study Priority Order

  1. Arithmetic first — Percentages, Profit & Loss, Time-Speed-Distance, Time & Work, Ratios, Averages, Mixtures, Simple & Compound Interest. This is your highest-return investment; it even overlaps with DILR data-heavy sets.
  2. Algebra next — Linear & Quadratic Equations, Functions, Inequalities, Logarithms, Indices, Surds, Sequences & Series.
  3. Geometry & Mensuration — Triangles, Circles, Quadrilaterals, Coordinate Geometry, 2D/3D Mensuration. Skip advanced Trigonometry initially — it's low-weightage (1-2 questions) and can be revisited later.
  4. Number System & Modern Maths last — Divisibility, Remainders, Factorials, Permutation & Combination, Probability, Set Theory. Fewer questions, but comparatively easier once fundamentals are strong.

MCQ vs TITA in QA

QA typically has a mix of 12-14 MCQs and 6-10 TITA questions, though the exact split varies each year. Since TITA has no negative marking (see our CAT Exam Pattern 2026 guide), it's worth attempting TITA questions even with moderate confidence — you lose nothing by trying.

Strategy for Non-Engineers

If you're from a non-engineering background, QA can feel intimidating — but Arithmetic alone can carry you to a 90+ percentile without needing advanced topics. Master Percentages, Ratios, Time & Work, and Profit & Loss thoroughly, and pair a moderate QA score with a strong VARC performance (a section non-engineers often do well in) for a solid overall percentile.

Frequently Asked Questions

What score in QA is good enough for a 99 percentile?

Roughly 28-30 marks out of a maximum of 66 (22 questions × 3 marks) is generally considered competitive for a 99 percentile, though this varies slightly by year based on overall difficulty.

Should I focus only on Arithmetic and Algebra?

They should get the majority of your prep time since they form 60-70% of the section, but don't skip Geometry, Number System, and Modern Maths entirely — those 4-5 questions can be the difference between a good and a great percentile.

Is CAT Quant difficult for non-engineering students?

Not necessarily. The syllabus traces back to Class 9-10 maths, and mastering just Arithmetic can secure a 90+ percentile without relying on advanced concepts.

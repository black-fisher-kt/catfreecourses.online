Best Books for CAT 2026: Section-Wise List for VARC, DILR & QA

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

CAT preparation isn't about collecting more books — it's about finishing one good book per section properly. Most aspirants own 4-5 books and complete none of them fully. Below is a focused, section-wise list along with how to actually use each one.

How to Use These Books Effectively

  1. Pick one trusted book per section — switching between multiple books on the same topic wastes time and creates confusion rather than adding value.
  2. Foundation phase (Months 1-3): Build concepts with basic-level books (NCERT, Level 1 Arun Sharma) and start vocabulary building.
  3. Application phase (Months 4-6): Move to Level 2-3 questions and advanced books like Quantum CAT once fundamentals are solid.
  4. Don't skip previous-year papers. No book, however good, replaces solving actual CAT PYQs to understand real question difficulty and style.
  5. Track completion, not collection. Finishing one book properly beats owning five and completing none.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Arun Sharma enough for CAT preparation?

Arun Sharma's books cover all three sections well and are widely used, but pair them with daily VARC reading and previous-year CAT papers for a complete preparation approach.

Should beginners start with NCERT books?

Yes, if your Class 9-10 maths fundamentals feel weak. NCERT is a good foundation before moving to CAT-specific books like Arun Sharma or Quantum CAT.

How many books do I really need for CAT?

One solid book per section (QA, VARC, DILR) plus previous-year question papers is typically enough. More books often reduce preparation quality rather than improving it.
